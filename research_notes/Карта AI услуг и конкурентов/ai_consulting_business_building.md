## 1. What AI services are businesses actually buying in 2025–2026? Ranked by demand and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Spending on AI services is very large and still growing. Gartner forecasts AI services at $436B in 2025 and $586B in 2026. Enterprises now **buy** most AI rather than build it: 76% purchased vs 24% built in 2025. So service demand is moving away from building models from scratch and toward integrating purchased tools, redesigning workflows, change management, and showing ROI. By money and by stated priority, the ranking is: (1) coding and IT tooling plus integration, (2) customer support/service automation, (3) back-office and process automation, which has the highest ROI per MIT, (4) vertical AI for healthcare and legal, (5) strategy/transformation programs at the top end, (6) training/upskilling and governance, which are fast-growing but have smaller tickets.

### Cited Findings
**Macro spend and services share**
- Gartner (May 2026) forecasts 2026 worldwide AI spending at ~$2.59T (+47% YoY). The **AI Services** line is $436.4B (2025) → $585.5B (2026) → $759.4B (2027) — [Gartner press release, 19 May 2026](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-19-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-ai-spending-to-grow-47-percent-in-2026). A later update in a single secondary source puts AI services at ~$576.5B for 2026 — [FourWeekMBA [SECONDARY]](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-gartner-only-1-of-a-2-67t-ai-bill-goes-to-the-models/).
- Gartner analyst John-David Lovelock calls 2026 the inflection year when spending shifts from tech companies/hyperscalers to enterprises — [Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-19-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-ai-spending-to-grow-47-percent-in-2026).
- Menlo Ventures (Dec 2025; ~495 US enterprise decision-makers) puts enterprise GenAI spend at **$37B in 2025**, up from $11.5B in 2024 (3.2x; the 2024 figure excludes inference). The application layer is $19B: horizontal $8.4B, departmental $7.3B, vertical $3.5B. The infrastructure layer is $18B, of which model APIs are $12.5B — [Menlo Ventures 2025 State of GenAI in the Enterprise](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/).
- **Menlo departmental split ($7.3B):** coding $4.0B (55%), IT ops ~$700M (10%), marketing ~$660M (9%), customer success ~$630M (9%), design 7%, HR 5% — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/).
- **Menlo vertical split ($3.5B):** healthcare ~$1.5B (ambient scribes ~$600M), legal ~$650M, creator tools ~$360M, government ~$350M — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/).
- **Buy vs build:** 76% purchased in 2025 vs 53% in 2024. AI deals reach production 47% of the time vs 25% for traditional SaaS. Product-led growth (PLG) accounts for 27% of AI application spend (vs 7% in traditional software) — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/).
- a16z CIO survey (100 Global-2000 CIOs, June 2025): GenAI budgets grow ~75% YoY, and spending is moving from innovation budgets into core IT/business-unit budgets (innovation share falls from 25% to 7%). >90% are testing third-party customer-support apps; regulated sectors still favour building in-house — [a16z](https://a16z.com/ai-enterprise-2025/); [SaaStr [SECONDARY]](https://www.saastr.com/a16z-enterprise-ai-spending-is-growing-75-a-year/). Average enterprise LLM spend: ~$2.5M (2024) → ~$7M (2025) → ~$11.6M expected for 2026 — [The Decoder [SECONDARY]](https://the-decoder.com/?p=31599).

**Adoption vs value (why services are bought)**
- McKinsey State of AI 2025 (survey May–July 2025, 1,993 respondents): only 39% attribute any EBIT impact to AI, mostly <5% of EBIT. 39% have begun experimenting with agents. In any given function, ≤10% are scaling agents. "High performers" (≥5% of EBIT from AI) are ~6%. Workflow redesign is among the factors most strongly linked to impact — [McKinsey 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025).
- McKinsey "State of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI" (released 25 Aug 2026; survey 4 May–8 Jun 2026; 1,719 respondents): 89% use AI in at least one function (88% in 2025); only **37%** report an EBIT contribution (essentially unchanged); 80% say AI improved their individual productivity — [The Next Web [SECONDARY]](https://thenextweb.com/news/mckinsey-ai-productivity-paradox-enterprise-roi-capex); [MarketScale [SECONDARY]](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/80-of-workers-say-ai-helps-but-only-37-see-ebit-impact.md); [digitalstrategy-ai [SECONDARY]](https://digitalstrategy-ai.com/mckinsey-state-of-ai-2026).
- BCG "Widening AI Value Gap" (Sept/Oct 2025; 1,250 execs): ~5% "future-built" capture value at scale, ~35% are scaling, ~60% get no material value. Agentic AI = 17% of AI value in 2025, projected 29% by 2028 — [BCG PDF](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-October-2025.pdf); [BCG press 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings).
- BCG Applied AI Index (30 Sept 2026; 1,330 CxOs): ~48% now generate value (7.5% future-built + 41% scaling). AI spend went from ~1.7% of revenue (late 2025) to **3.3%**, with **>80% of it outside the IT budget**. Agentic AI is expected to be ~40% of AI value by 2030. 42% expect to give agents real decision authority by 2030, but only **5% have the full set of controls** today. Dedicated AI roles are projected to grow from 7% to 22% of the workforce. 7 in 10 leaders/scalers are already retraining staff — [BCG press 2026](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2026-ai-starting-to-pay-off-companies-generate-value).
- MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" (2025; 150 interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 deployments): 95% of organisations report no measurable return. More than half of GenAI budgets go to sales/marketing tools, yet the biggest ROI is in **back-office automation**. Externally partnered builds succeed ~67% of the time vs ~1/3 as often for internal builds (self-reported; definitions vary) — [Fortune via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/mit-report-95-generative-ai-105412686.html); critique of the 95% headline: [arnon.dk](https://arnon.dk/mits-95-ai-failure-rate-is-wrong/).

**Big-firm demand signals**
- Accenture FY2025 (to 31 Aug 2025): **$5.9B** GenAI/"advanced AI" new bookings (Q4: $1.8B), roughly double the prior year. Advanced AI revenue tripled to **$2.7B**. These figures exclude data, classical AI and AI used in delivery — [Accenture Q4 FY25 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737325000213/q4fy25earnings8-kexhibit.htm); [Accenture 2025 shareholder letter](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/home/accenture-2025-letter-to-shareholders.pdf).
- IBM: GenAI "book of business" was ~$9.5B at Q3 2025 — [CRN Asia](https://www.crnasia.com/news-network/2025/ibm-9) — and ~$12.5B by end-2025, with consulting signings >$10.5B (~80%) — [MarketMinute [SECONDARY]](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/marketminute-2026-1-28-ibms-ai-transformation-crystallizes-q4-earnings-surpass-expectations-as-generative-ai-book-hits-12-billion).

**SMB demand (US-heavy; many secondary)**
- US SMB adoption figures depend heavily on definition: Goldman Sachs 10KSB Voices (early 2026, 1,256 owners) 76% "use AI in some form"; Fed Small Business Credit Survey 46% of small employer firms (+15% planning); Small Business Expo survey 71.4%. Top uses: marketing 45%, customer service 37%, bookkeeping 35% (one source), or data analysis 26.8%, marketing materials 25%, emails 23.4% (ICIC/Intuit) — compiled in [Capsule CRM [SECONDARY]](https://capsulecrm.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-statistics/), [Lilach Bullock [SECONDARY]](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ai-adoption-statistics-small-business/), [Simply Business 2026 outlook](https://www.simplybusiness.com/small-businesses-are-using-ai-but-theyre-not-letting-it-run-the-show-2026-outlook/). Salesforce-attributed: 93% plan to keep investing, 62% to increase budgets [SECONDARY, same compilations].
- Upwork (freelance-market proxy for SMB/startup buying): GSV from AI-related work grew 53% YoY in Q3 2025 (30% in Q2) — [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4513834-upwork-signals-acceleration-in-gsv-and-revenue-growth-into-2026-as-ai-related-projects-surge) — and exceeded **$300M annualised** in Q4 2025 (+50%), while total Upwork revenue grew only 2% to $787.8M and active clients fell 6% — [Upwork FY2025 results via Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/114482/upwork-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial-results). Clients hiring for AI work grew 109% YoY, "led by video and integration work" — [SelfEmployed](https://www.selfemployed.com/?p=97290). AI freelancers earn a >40% hourly premium (Q1 2025) — [CXR / Upwork Q1 2025](https://community.cxr.works/blogs/rachel-noto/2025/05/06/upwork-q1-2025-revenue-bucks-client-decline-to-inc).

### Inferences
- **[EST] Demand ranking (volume × willingness to pay), excluding marketing/messaging/drafting/video:**
  1. **Transformation/implementation programs at enterprise level.** Highest willingness to pay (Accenture/IBM bookings in the billions). Effectively closed to a one-person studio.
  2. **Customer-service/support automation and AI front-desk agents.** Broadest demand from enterprises (a16z >90% testing) through SMBs (US 37% customer service; Israel CBS 35%). Tickets are mid-sized and productised competitors (SaaS) are many.
  3. **Back-office/process automation** (finance ops, document handling, scheduling, CRM/ERP glue). Highest proven ROI per MIT, and the main use category among AI-using Israeli businesses (63%, see Q4). This is the best fit for an engineer-led studio.
  4. **Coding/IT and internal-tool builds.** Biggest departmental spend ($4B), but mostly bought as SaaS (Cursor etc.). The services opportunity is custom internal apps and integrations.
  5. **Vertical AI (healthcare, legal).** Real money ($1.5B, $650M) but dominated by funded products. The studio's role is configuring and integrating those products for clinics and firms, not competing with them.
  6. **Training/upskilling and governance.** Growing quickly (BCG: 7 in 10 retraining; only 5% have agent controls) at lower ticket size. Good as a door-opener.
- Because 76% of AI is bought and >80% of AI spend sits outside IT, the buyer for a small studio is usually a **business-unit owner or SMB owner, not the CIO**. That favours fast, outcome-framed offers.

### Gaps
- No primary 2026 SMB survey with **dollar spend on external AI services** (as opposed to tools) was found.
- I could not open the full McKinsey 2026 report. Its figures come from secondary coverage.
- No independent data was found ranking willingness to pay by service type for SMBs.

## 2. Who sells what, at what price: global giants vs boutiques vs AI automation agencies vs "AI employee" SaaS vs freelancers

### Takeaway
Prices fall into tiers that differ by orders of magnitude. Big firms charge **$300K–$1.5M for strategy and $3–10M+ for implementation** and increasingly tie fees to outcomes. Boutiques charge **$15K–$500K**. Automation agencies charge **$1.5K–$12K per build plus $500–$15K/month retainers**. Custom SMB agents cost **$10K–$80K**. Freelancers charge **~$90–$235/h** or $150–$3,000 per gig. At the bottom, "AI employee" SaaS sells for **$50–$600/month** (Artisan, Lindy, Sintra) up to $3,750/month (11x). That SaaS tier caps what an SMB will pay for commodity bots. Almost all mid- and low-tier price data comes from vendor blogs, so treat it as indicative.

### Cited Findings
**Global giants (Accenture, Deloitte, McKinsey/QuantumBlack, BCG X, IBM)**
- Scale: Accenture $5.9B GenAI bookings and $2.7B revenue (FY25) — [Accenture 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737325000213/q4fy25earnings8-kexhibit.htm). IBM ~$12.5B GenAI book, ~80% of it consulting — [MarketMinute [SECONDARY]](https://business.mammothtimes.com/mammothtimes/article/marketminute-2026-1-28-ibms-ai-transformation-crystallizes-q4-earnings-surpass-expectations-as-generative-ai-book-hits-12-billion).
- McKinsey: about **one quarter of global fees are now outcome-based** (disclosed at a Nov 2025 London briefing). Consulting firms from BCG to Accenture are moving more projects to outcome-based fees — [Hunt Scanlon](https://huntscanlon.com/mckinsey-continues-to-deliver-value-it-just-charges-differently-for-it-now/); [Yahoo Finance / Business Insider](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-reshaping-mckinsey-makes-money-195132745.html); [Yahoo: AI forcing McKinsey, BCG, Bain to rethink fees](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/ai-forcing-mckinsey-bcg-bain-093300265.html). A vendor cost guide claims 73% of consulting buyers favour outcome-tied pricing — [aidirectory [VENDOR/SECONDARY]](https://aidirectory.com/news/ai-pushes-consultancies-toward-outcome-based-fees).
- Price levels (no official rate cards exist; these are secondhand estimates that conflict). Strategy-house AI work runs **$400–$900/h**. McKinsey/BCG senior partners bill **$1,100–$1,200/h**; implementation analysts **$327–$498/h**. An AI strategy engagement alone costs **$300K–$1.5M**. Big 4: ~$500K for strategy and **$3–10M for implementation**, over 3–6 months for strategy. Enterprise AI transformation runs $1–5M+ — compiled across [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-much-ai-consultant-costs/), [Alice Labs](https://alicelabs.ai/en/insights/ai-consulting-pricing-2026), [Globy](https://gogloby.com/insights/ai-consulting-cost/), [ThinkingInc [VENDOR]](https://thinkinginc.pages.dev/en/boss-articles/alternatives-to-big-4-ai-consulting/), [Dan Cumberland Labs [VENDOR]](https://dancumberlandlabs.com/blog/top-ai-consulting-firms/). I could not map each number to a single page.

**Boutiques / mid-market AI consultancies**
- Full engagement **$75K–$500K**. Readiness assessment **$15K–$60K over 3–6 weeks**. UK example: £15K–£80K for an audit plus a 12-week sprint — same compilation as above ([Alice Labs](https://alicelabs.ai/en/insights/ai-consulting-pricing-2026), [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-much-ai-consultant-costs/), [ThinkingInc [VENDOR]](https://thinkinginc.pages.dev/en/boss-articles/ai-consulting-for-mid-market/)).
- Hourly benchmarks: junior AI consultant $100–$150/h, senior $300–$500+/h — [Orient Software [VENDOR]](https://www.orientsoftware.com/blog/ai-consultant-hourly-rate/).

**AI automation agencies (n8n/Make/Zapier builders, chatbot/agent shops)**
- The dominant model is a **setup fee plus a monthly retainer**. Basic automation build: **$1,500–$12,000** one-off. Small-business retainers start around **$500/month**. Full-service retainers run **$3K–$15K/month** (another source: $4K–$12K). Mid-market setups cost $20K+ and enterprise builds can exceed $100K. Integration complexity, not workflow length, is the main cost driver. Sample offer: **$3,500 setup + $450/month** "AI ops retainer", with software at cost +15% — [AutomateNexus [VENDOR]](https://automatenexus.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-pricing-complete-cost-guide-2025); [Codewave [VENDOR]](https://codewave.com/feeds/blog/ai-automation-agency-pricing); [DEV: 2026 pricing reality [VENDOR]](https://dev.to/jahanzaibai/what-do-ai-automation-services-actually-cost-the-2026-pricing-reality-595k); [Ciela [VENDOR]](https://ciela.ai/blogs/what-to-charge-for-ai-automation-services).
- Solo automation operators charge **$95–$235/h** for n8n/Make/Zapier work in 2026 — [Vantaige 2026 rate card [VENDOR]](https://vantaige.io/zh/blog/2026-ai-automation-rate-card-operators-charge). Claimed agency monthly churn is 2–5% with 18–30+ month lifetimes, unsourced — [Ciela [VENDOR]](https://ciela.ai/blogs/ai-agency-client-retention).

**Custom AI agents / AI apps**
- SMB custom builds mostly cost **$20K–$80K**. A single-task prototype costs **$10K–$30K**. Discovery/system design is $5K–$20K. Base agent development is $12K–$18K plus features. Narrowing v1 scope cuts cost 30–50%. Complex multi-agent systems run >$75K, and enterprise platforms >$1M — [Layer3Labs [VENDOR]](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-agent-development-for-small-business); [Cleveroad [VENDOR]](https://www.cleveroad.com/blog/ai-agent-development-cost/); [Biz4Group "$20K to $60K" [VENDOR]](https://www.biz4group.com/blog/ai-agent-development-cost); [DEV "$500 to $150K" [VENDOR]](https://dev.to/pat9000/ai-agent-cost-in-2026-500-to-150k-real-pricing-breakdown-1m12).

**Freelancers / marketplaces**
- Fiverr/Contra n8n and agent gigs: from ~$200 (7 days), $750 for an end-to-end build (2 weeks), and ~$3,000 for a custom project. WhatsApp bots run $150–$800 — e.g., [Contra n8n build](https://contra.com/s/cMmlWEMA-custom-ai-automation-build-n8n), [Fiverr n8n agents](https://fiverr.com/alee_imam/build-n8n-ai-agents-and-automations), [Fiverr WhatsApp support agent](https://fiverr.com/sashanclrp/build-your-whatsapp-ai-customer-support-agent). Regie.ai $180–$499/user/month — [Layer3Labs comparison [VENDOR]](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-employee-platforms-compared); [Artisan on AiSDR](https://www.artisan.co/blog/aisdr-pricing-and-reviews).

### Inferences
- **[EST] Where a one-person studio can compete:**
  - Fixed-scope "audit → pilot → retainer" offers in the $2K–$25K band for SMBs and mid-market teams, which is below boutique minimums ($15K+ assessments) and well below Big 4.
  - Custom internal apps and agents where an off-the-shelf "AI employee" doesn't fit: Hebrew, local integrations, regulated data, odd workflows.
  - **Productised verticals.** Engineer/inventor credibility suits manufacturing, product companies, e-commerce/Amazon ops, and IP-heavy firms.
- **[EST] Where to avoid:**
  - Enterprise multi-year transformations, which need bench depth and procurement standing.
  - Commodity chatbots priced against $50–$600/month SaaS.
  - SDR/"AI employee" outbound, where 11x/Artisan-type products and marketing exclusions overlap.
  - Competing on hourly price with Fiverr ($150–$800 bots).
- Big firms moving to **outcome-based fees** (~25% at McKinsey) normalises "pay for results" for buyers. A small studio can copy it at small scale (e.g., a base fee plus a bonus tied to measured hours or tickets saved).

### Gaps
- No published, verifiable rate cards for Deloitte/BCG X/QuantumBlack AI engagements. All figures are secondhand.
- No independent survey of AI-agency revenue distribution (median agency size, client count). Reddit/IndieHackers first-hand threads did not surface in search.
- The claim that AI accounts for ~40% of McKinsey's work (attributed to QuantumBlack co-lead Alex Singla) appeared in search but could not be tied to a verifiable page.
- I did not get Deloitte, Cognizant, or Infosys AI-services revenue figures.

## 3. Venture studios / business-building services for founders and SMBs ("idea-to-business", MVP, brand + launch, Amazon/e-commerce launch)

### Takeaway
There are three business models, with very different risk:
- **Equity venture studios.** The studio typically takes 15–80% of the company (average ~34%). Its self-reported numbers are strong (84% of studio startups raise a seed round vs 42% of other startups; 53% IRR). They are unaudited and subject to survivorship bias.
- **Fixed-fee MVP/launch agencies.** $3K–$150K per MVP. An AI-integrated MVP costs ~£20K–£50K over 8–16 weeks.
- **Percent-of-revenue e-commerce/Amazon agencies.** Retainers of $1.5K–$25K/month, or 3–15% of Amazon sales, plus launch packages from ~$2.5K.

For a solo studio, a **hybrid model is realistic**: a fixed fee for validation and MVP, plus a small equity stake or revenue share. Pure equity studios need a fund behind them.

### Cited Findings
**Venture studios (equity model)**
- GSSN data, cited secondhand: ~84% of studio startups raise seed and 72% reach Series A, vs 42% for traditional startups. Average IRR is 53% vs 21.3%. Studio equity stakes run 15–80% (average ~34%). A 2020 GSSN study of 23 studios / 415 companies found ~9% failed, 3% exited, 88% active — [Bundl](https://bundl.com/articles/why-venture-studio-startups-have-higher-long-term-success-rates); [High Alpha [studio, self-interested]](https://highalpha.com/why-venture-studios-are-the-future-of-company-building/); [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/alternative-investments/how-venture-studios-build-startups); [Ecosistema Startup](https://ecosistemastartup.com/venture-building-53-irr-vs-21-vc-tradicional-en-startups/). Caveat: industry-reported, the primary GSSN report was not reviewed, and survivorship bias and the IRR method are unknown.
- Studio revenue comes from equity gains, service fees to portfolio companies, fund management fees, and carry (20–30%). A practitioner puts typical studio equity at 20–60% — [Ecosistema Startup](https://ecosistemastartup.com/venture-building-53-irr-vs-21-vc-tradicional-en-startups/); [Réseau Capital](https://reseaucapital.com/en/8-advantages-of-venture-studios/).
- Israel's flagship studio, Team8 (founded 2014 by Unit 8200 veterans), uses a "foundry" model with 90+ in-house company builders and a C-level network. It has built **30 companies** from scratch, and portfolio companies raised **$762M in 2025** — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/team8-foundry-venture-model-profile/); [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/business/article/rkumswzx11x); background: [Globes "Team8: A unique model, but will it work?"](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001292595). Founder-equity terms were not found.

**Fixed-fee MVP / build agencies**
- MVP cost in 2026 ranges from $3K–$80K in one guide to $15K–$150K+ in another. A simple single-platform MVP costs $10K–$20K. UK: £10K–£25K for most MVPs. An **AI-integrated MVP (LLM, RAG) costs £20K–£50K over 8–16 weeks**. A discovery sprint takes 2 weeks and costs from £3K. Fixed price typically runs 10–15% above time-and-materials (citing Clutch). Watch for change orders outside the spec — [Empat [VENDOR]](https://www.empat.tech/blog/mvp-development-cost); [Magora UK [VENDOR]](https://magora-systems.com/mvp-cost-uk/); [Hapy [VENDOR]](https://hapy.co/journal/mvp-development-cost-in-2026-what-founders-should-budget-before-version-one/); [RaftLabs [VENDOR]](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/complete-mvp-development-cost).
- "A $5,000 project built on Lovable and a $150,000 project built by a development agency aren't the same product at different price points" — [Kaizen [VENDOR]](https://kaizen-website-eks.pages.dev/blog/taste-and-judgment/real-cost-of-mvp).

**Amazon / e-commerce launch services**
- Amazon agency retainers run **$1,500–$25,000+/month**, or a narrower $2,500–$15,000. Percent-of-sales models take **3–15%** of monthly Amazon revenue (other sources: 3–10%, 3–8%). Hybrid example: $3,500/month base + 4% of sales above $400K. Ad-spend models take 10–20% of spend, which misaligns incentives. "Sales" must be defined in the contract (ordered/shipped/gross/net) — [SupplyKick [VENDOR]](https://www.supplykick.com/blog/amazon-agency-pricing); [amzDUDES [VENDOR]](https://amzdudes.com/amazon-agency-pricing/); [EcomBrainly](https://ecombrainly.com/amazon-advertising-agencies/); [Gigabrands [VENDOR]](https://gigabrands.ai/blog/amazon-agency-cost-breakdown).
- Published launch package: **$2,495** (copy, 5 photos, 20-second video, PPC setup) plus launch PPC management at **$995/month for 60 days**. Agency fees are only part of launch cost; ads, inventory and fees come on top — [SalesDuo [VENDOR]](https://salesduo.com/blog/amazon-product-launch-service-cost).
- Low end: Fiverr "full Amazon FBA private label launch A to Z" gigs exist — [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com/vifalu/launch-your-amazon-fba-project-from-scratch).

### Inferences
- **[EST] Leon's edge.** Few AI studios can credibly say they have **built an Amazon brand from zero, hold 10+ design patents, and run an e-commerce store**. That supports a productised "idea → validated product → patent/design → Amazon launch" track for founders and SMBs, priced as fixed-fee stages:
  1. Validation sprint (≈$1.5–5K / ₪5–18K).
  2. Design/MVP/listing (≈$5–25K).
  3. Optional revenue share (3–8%, matching Amazon-agency norms) or small equity in place of part of the fee.
- **[EST] Risks.**
  - Equity-for-services from a solo operator ties up cash-flow capacity. Venture-studio success stats are self-reported and come from funded, staffed studios.
  - Founder clients have high failure base rates.
  - Revenue share needs tight definitions of "sales" and audit rights.
  - Avoid open-ended "we build your business" promises. Sell stage gates with kill criteria.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-GSSN) data was found on venture-studio success rates, or on failure rates for "done-for-you business" or Amazon-launch clients.
- No public data was found on SMB-focused (non-VC) venture-building services such as "business-from-zero" packages or their pricing; providers mostly quote privately.
- Israeli venture-studio fee/equity terms (Team8 and smaller studios) were not found.

## 4. Israeli market specifics: adoption, programs/grants, players, ILS prices

### Takeaway
Israeli business adoption is above the EU average but shallow outside tech. In June 2025, 28% of businesses with 10+ employees used AI, vs 13–15% in the EU (2024 figure). Only ~16% of businesses with ≤50 employees pay for AI. The 2026 CBS wave shows fast growth in manufacturing and a **doubling of "lack of knowledge" as a barrier (8% → 16%)**, which points directly to demand for training and implementation.

On funding, there is a **new SMB Agency grant (July 2026: 35% of investment, up to ₪350K, turnover ≤₪100M) covering AI-tool integration and automation**. The Innovation Authority's AI money mostly targets R&D/high-tech, plus reservist AI-champion training. Local SMB prices are low:
- WhatsApp/AI bots: ~$1K–$2K setup plus ₪100–₪500/month.
- AI-agent SaaS: ₪500–₪4,000/month.
- Custom builds: ₪5K–₪20K.
- Workshop seats: ~₪1,200 + VAT per participant.

### Cited Findings
**Adoption data (CBS = Israel Central Bureau of Statistics)**

## 5. Emerging / niche AI services with real demand, and the most profitable verticals for a small studio

### Takeaway
The niches with real evidence of demand are:
- **Back-office/process automation and document workflows** (highest ROI per MIT; 63% of Israeli AI users apply AI to management/business processes).
- **AI front-desk/customer-service agents** for clinics and service businesses.
- **AI governance/controls** (only 5% of firms have full agent controls; ISO 42001 demand is customer-driven).
- **Team AI training** (knowledge-gap barrier doubling; BCG retraining).
- **AI search visibility (GEO)**, which has hot demand but is marketing-adjacent.

Vertical AI money is largest in healthcare and legal, but funded products dominate it. A small studio's profitable role is **integration and configuration around those products** and **underserved traditional industries** (manufacturing, product/e-commerce companies, professional services).

## 6. Failure modes, buyer complaints, and what proof/guarantees win deals

### Takeaway
"Pilot purgatory" is well documented:
- 95% of firms report no measurable GenAI return (MIT; contested).
- Only 37–39% see any EBIT impact (McKinsey 2025/2026).
- >40% of agentic projects are forecast to be cancelled by 2027, amid widespread "agent washing" (Gartner).

Clients churn from agencies mainly because **ROI isn't demonstrated**. What wins deals is narrow scope tied to a measured workflow, external-partner delivery (67% vs ~33% success), outcome-linked fees, P&L-level measurement, production references that have run 6–12 months, and clear terms on ownership and support.

### Cited Findings
- **Failure rates:**
  - MIT NANDA: 95% no measurable return, blamed on a "learning gap"/integration rather than model quality. External partnerships succeed ~67% vs ~1/3 for internal builds — [Yahoo/Fortune](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/mit-report-95-generative-ai-105412686.html). A critic argues the 95% is overstated (citing 25–30% failure for intentional production deployments; a single vendor's claim) — [arnon.dk](https://arnon.dk/mits-95-ai-failure-rate-is-wrong/).
  - Gartner (25 Jun 2025): >40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end-2027 due to cost, unclear value or inadequate risk controls. Only ~130 of "thousands" of agentic vendors are genuine ("agent washing" of assistants, RPA and chatbots). In a Jan 2025 poll, 19% had made significant agentic investments and 42% were conservative — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027).

### Inferences
- **[EST] Deal-winning package for a solo studio:**
  1. A paid diagnostic with a baseline metric (hours, response time, error rate, ₪/month).
  2. A fixed-price pilot on **one** workflow, with a go/no-go criterion.
  3. A retainer only after the metric moves. Optionally, part of the fee tied to verified savings (mirroring McKinsey's ~25%).
  4. Written terms: client owns code, data and accounts. Running costs (API, WhatsApp, n8n) are itemised. A 30-day hypercare period. Monthly ROI report (directly addresses the #1 churn cause).
- **[EST] Credibility assets that matter for Leon:**
  - Real production references, especially his own e-commerce store run on his own automations ("we run this ourselves").
  - The patents and the Amazon brand as proof of building something from zero.
  - Honest anti-agent-washing positioning: say what is an LLM workflow vs a true agent.
- Avoid promising headcount replacement. Israeli data show 89% of AI-using firms report no employment effect (CBS 2025), and vendors concede full front-desk replacement is unrealistic. Frame the offer as capacity and response-time gains.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) survey of SMB buyer complaints about AI agencies or freelancers was found. Reddit/IndieHackers first-hand data did not surface.
- No data on the effectiveness of specific guarantees (money-back, pay-on-results) in AI services sales.
